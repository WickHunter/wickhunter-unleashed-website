Liq Scalper

Fade large liquidations when price is stretched outside a band you set.

Liq Scalper and Optimized Liquidation Bot | Course 45:22 · Watch on YouTube ↗Liq Scalper: Every Setting Explained | Wick Hunter11:35 · Watch on YouTube ↗

The Liq Scalper takes the other side of forced liquidations: it buys panic selling and sells panic buying. It enters only when both are true at the same moment:

  1. A liquidation at least your Min liquidation size prints on the side you fade.
  2. Price is outside your band around the VWMA fair-value line (Long band · % below VWMA, Short band · % above VWMA).

The liquidation feed only tells the bot which coin. Prices, candles and orders always come from the exchange you trade. Bybit's feed is the most complete; Binance and OKX feeds are sampled or partial, so treat them as extra hints, not as a complete picture.

Recommended starter configuration

This is a suggestion, not a default. New forms start blank.

Liquidation sourcesBybit USDT (Binance USDT on a Binance account)
PairsA manual list of 3–5 liquid pairs
DirectionBoth, with Max longs 1, Max shorts 1, Max both 1
Min liquidation sizeHigh enough to ignore noise, e.g. 1,000 USD
Long / Short band2%
Base orderDollar, a small amount above the exchange minimum
Leverage3–5×
DCA orders0 to start (type 0; the − button stops at 1)
Take profitFixed %, Target profit about 1%
Stop lossOn, Offset from Initial entry price

Key settings

  • Base order is position size, not margin. $200 at 10× is a $200 position using about $20 of margin.
  • DCA ladder levels are measured from the original entry and fill on touch. Nothing caps a deal's size except the ladder you set. The form's % of balance used · if every DCA fills shows your worst case.
  • Stop loss is off unless you switch it on. Put it beyond your deepest DCA level, or the deep levels never fill.
  • Min liquidation size has three modes. Dollar floor: the print must be at least this many USD. Pair percentile: the print must be big for that pair, compared with its own recent liquidations. Floor + percentile: the print must pass both, so a tiny print never trades even on the smallest pair. A pair with no liquidation history yet is refused, never guessed; the signal rail says which threshold failed.
  • Minimum time between new entries (seconds, blank or 0 = off): after the bot opens a deal, it opens no other new deal, on any pair, until this time has passed. The signal rail shows "Entry cooldown: next entry allowed in Xm Ys." Open deals' DCAs, take profits, closes and hedges are unaffected; an unfilled entry does not start the wait; the wait survives a restart.
  • Cooldown after Stop Loss (Pair / Bot Cooldown, minutes) needs the stop loss switched on.
  • Pair filters (24h volume, Volatility, Leverage floor, Minimum coin market cap, blacklists) only block new entries. For the market-cap filter, blank or 0 = off, and shorthand such as 250M is accepted. The leverage floor reads each pair's maximum on the exchange this bot trades; where a maximum is not published the pair shows ?× and the app invents no limit.

Common mistakes

  • High leverage plus a deep ladder and no stop: the exchange liquidates you before the ladder finishes.
  • A band so tight that every small dip triggers an entry.
  • Raising Max longs/shorts: one market-wide flush then opens several correlated deals together.
  • Reading % sizes without checking the unit next to the box.

Why didn't it trade?

Open Activity or the bot's signal rail. Common reasons: price inside the band, print below the minimum size (or the pair percentile), an entry cooldown still running, deal caps full, pair filtered out, stale candle data ("Entry data refused"), or a Market limit reached.
